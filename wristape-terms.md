Wristape records what its microphone hears, which can include other people. Whether you may record a conversation, and whether you need everyone's consent first, depends on where you are and who you're recording: in many places recording a conversation without the consent of everyone in it is against the law, and some places, such as clinics, workplaces, schools and courts, have rules of their own.

- what you do with recordings, transcripts and notes of other people, including sharing them.

Wristape gives you ways to do this (it records only when you start it, shows that it's recording, pauses, stops at places you choose, and deletes anything), but it can't know the law where you are or whether people have agreed. The developer is not responsible for recordings made, kept or shared against the law or against anyone's wishes.

## 2. What the app writes may be wrong

Transcripts, the telling apart of voices, reviews, meeting notes, to-dos, reminders, memories, goals and patterns, suggestions, answers and day pictures are made automatically, by speech recognition and by AI models, on your iPhone or by a service you choose. They can be incomplete, misheard, attributed to the wrong person, out of date or simply wrong, and a reminder can be late, early or missing.

Check anything that matters against the recording before relying on it. Don't rely on Wristape alone for anything where a mistake could cause harm.

Nothing in Wristape is medical, health, legal, financial, psychological or other professional advice, even when it summarizes what a doctor, lawyer or adviser said, or suggests what to do. Wristape is not a medical device and is not for emergencies: if you need help, contact the emergency services or a professional.

Protect your iPhone with a passcode; Wristape can also be locked with Face ID.
